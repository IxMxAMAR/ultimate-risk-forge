# Architecture

Updated as modules land.

## 1. What it is
An offline browser app (no server, no network requests) for practising risk assessment on generated, fictional seller cases.

## 2. Stack
TypeScript, React and Vite. Vitest with Testing Library for tests. IndexedDB (via `idb`) for persistence. Fonts are bundled from `@fontsource/*`.

## 3. Layout
`src/app` holds the shell, `src/styles` the design tokens and global CSS, `src/test` the test setup. `src/domain` holds the types, case codes, rules and labels; `src/forge` the case generators and starter codes; `src/checks` the dispute math, flags, exposure and suggestions; `src/report` the report model and consistency; `src/practice` the scoring; `src/persist` the IndexedDB store; `src/lib` the small helpers (codes, money, dates); `src/ui` the console, report and forge screens; `scripts` the offline check and the screenshot tool.

## 4. Domain core
Pure types and logic for sellers, transactions, evidence and cases. Money is integer cents; dates are UTC ISO strings.

Types live in `src/domain/types.ts` (case specs, payments, disputes, refunds, holds, flags, decisions, ground truth, forged cases). The thresholds every check uses are one frozen ruleset, `RULES_V1` in `src/domain/ruleset.ts` (network limits, Mastercard fine schedules, hold buffer and rounding, young-account and escalation cutoffs).

The forge (`src/forge`) turns a case code into a full case: `forgeCase(code)` decodes it, picks the generator and seeds it with the case seed. Available types are Hidden by growth and Surprise (Surprise draws a concrete available type from the seed XOR 0x9E3779B9 and is flagged `surprise`); other types throw "This kind of case is coming soon". The Hidden by growth generator (`generators/hiddenByGrowth.ts`) builds card payments for Jun–Oct 2026 (October volume 300 / 2,000 / 20,000 by seller size, September = October ÷ growth of 6 / 5 / 4 for easy / normal / hard, earlier months flat-ish), brand-group dispute rates tuned per difficulty on the judged months (Jun–Aug; counts are rounded; because rounding on small sellers can land under the line, Visa and Mastercard are given at least enough disputes to end over their own limit (1.5% each) — so the over-limit flags are guaranteed for any seller with 30+ payments on the brand, and calibration separately checks that medium sellers stay near the tuned rates; "other" cards run at 2.5% on easy and 2% on normal) with low so-far rates on Sep/Oct (Sep 0.8% / 0.6% / 0.4% and Oct 0.2% / 0.15% / 0.1% for easy / normal / hard; Visa is 2.7% on easy; Mastercard is 4.2% / 3.1% / 3.2% for easy / normal / hard, so medium sellers always land at or above the 3.0% excessive tier and the Mastercard flag is critical at every difficulty), refunds on about 0.6% of payments (never on disputed ones), a fixed hold of 22.5% of the last 30 days rounded up to $1,000, and the ground truth. Hard cases add an ad-campaign receipts document and treat the young-account flag as a look-alike. Seller names come from `names.ts`; `starters.ts` lists the three starter codes (easy, normal, hard).

`src/app/derive.ts` turns a stored `CaseState` into a `CaseView`: the forged case, dispute stats, rule flags and exposure (computed once per code and reused), the flag list (rule flags with the analyst's states and notes merged in, then analyst-added flags) and the suggested decision from those flags.

## 5. Checks & rules
Deterministic checks over a case that produce severity-ranked flags with supporting evidence.

Dispute math (`src/checks/disputeMath.ts`): `disputeStats(case, rules)` computes the 30-day dispute rate (window `(asOf − 30d, asOf]`, payments by `ts`, disputes by `filedTs`) and a per-month table from the earliest payment month through the month before asOf's month, including empty months (rate NaN, shown as "—"). A month is judged once its last day is on or before asOf − 60 days; judged rates count disputes by the payment's month, while filed-month counts feed the "Last 30 days" chart mode. Judged totals also give per-brand rows (visa, mastercard, amex, discover; `brandGroup` folds amex and discover into "other"), dispute reasons, won/lost/open outcomes, the average judged dispute amount (whole dollars), refund rate, open disputes, growth (last full month's payments ÷ the month before) and immature payments. `worstBrandRate` returns the higher of visa and mastercard, each only with at least 30 judged payments. Rates with no payments are NaN, never Infinity. All date math is UTC.

Money at risk (`src/checks/exposure.ts`): `exposure(stats, rules)` adds up open disputes, expected new disputes on the immature months, assumed dispute fees ($15 each) and Mastercard fines into a low–high range. Expected disputes are immature payments × the judged rate (low) or × the worst brand rate when it is higher (high), valued at the average judged dispute amount. `networkFines` reads the judged Mastercard rate (needs 30 payments): at 1.5% it uses the ECM ladder, at 3.0% the HECM ladder, low = month 2 fine, high = months 2 and 3; below 1.5% there is no fines row. `holdToKeep` is the worst case × 1.05 rounded up to $1,000; `coverage` says whether a hold covers the worst case and by what percent (a worst case of 0 or less is always covered, with a 0% buffer). Callers pass the smaller of the worst case and the held amount, because no hold can exceed what is held.

Flags (`src/checks/flags.ts`): `runChecks(case, stats, rules)` raises five rule flags (state raised, added by rules, id = kind, evidence tab Disputes), sorted critical → high → medium and then by `FLAG_ORDER`. Hidden by growth (critical): growth ≥ 3×, judged rate ≥ 2× the 30-day rate and ≥ 1.5%. Mastercard over limit (high, critical at twice the 1.5% line) and Visa over limit (high, critical at twice 1.5%) use the judged brand rate and need 30 judged payments for that brand. Refunds low, disputes high (medium): refund rate under half the judged rate, judged rate ≥ 1%. Young account, fast growth (medium): account ≤ 6 months old and growth ≥ 3×. A kind listed as a look-alike in the ground truth is capped at medium. `escalate` moves a severity one step up when the value reaches twice its threshold; `reviewDateFrom` gives the first 15th on or after asOf + 30 days.

Suggested decision (`src/checks/suggest.ts`): `suggestDecision` looks only at confirmed flags. Two or more critical → deny and keep everything held; one critical or any high → partial release, keeping the lesser of the held amount and the hold to keep for the high money-at-risk figure; otherwise release the hold, reviewed on asOf. Action and reason names shown to the user live in `src/domain/labels.ts`.

Practice score (`src/practice/score.ts`): `scoreCase(case, ruleFlags, analystFlags, decision, worst)` scores out of 100. Flags 40: each planted kind earns an equal share when its rule flag is confirmed or an analyst flag's evidence ids overlap the rule flag's; each confirmed look-alike costs half a share; clamped 0–40. Dismissals 15: the share of look-alikes dismissed (or, with no look-alikes, one minus the share of planted flags dismissed). Decision 30: an acceptable action scores 30, an adjacent one 15. Amounts 15: the kept amount covers the worst case (or the whole hold, when the worst case is larger) and is not more than the held amount. A dismissed analyst flag earns no Flags credit. The total is rounded; `missed` and `wronglyConfirmed` list flag titles.

Same facts, same answer (`src/report/consistency.ts`): `consistency(current, past)` compares the kept share of the hold with the median of past decisions (in line within 10 points) and gives the min–max kept share among past decisions with the same action, falling back to all. `REFERENCE_DECISIONS` holds four reference decisions for Hidden by growth.

Report model (`src/report/buildReport.ts`, wording in `src/report/templates.ts`): `buildReport(view, caseState, analyst, rules)` returns everything the report page shows. The decision is the saved one, or the suggestion while undecided (status Draft until a decision is saved). The override box appears only when the saved action or kept amount differs from the suggestion, with the analyst's reason. Confidence is high once no flag is still raised. The money section reuses the exposure rows and says whether the kept amount covers the worst case and by what percent; the conditions, fallback date and "how this was estimated" text are filled from the case's own numbers (the top judged dispute reason decides whether the second condition is 3D Secure). The seller message is a plain-language letter whose money sentence comes from the decision's `keep` and `release` ("all of it" only when everything is released, "the full $X" only when everything is kept, otherwise both amounts); an edited message replaces it. Attachments are listed by page, and the consistency block compares the kept share with the reference decisions (in line when no references exist).

Playbook engine (`src/playbooks/engine.ts`, types in `types.ts`): a playbook is an ordered list of steps; each step has a question, data to look at, answers and the answers the data supports. `walk(playbook, view, answers)` marks each step done, current, upcoming or skipped (a step is skipped when its `when` returns false) and flags drift when a done step's effects no longer match the live flag states. `effectsOf` turns chosen answers into flag states: the chosen answers' `sets` apply, every other kind the step mentions is dismissed, and a neutral answer changes nothing. `answerStep` stores an answer, clears answers of later steps that become skipped and returns the flag states to write; flags only a cleared step touched go back to raised. `expectedPath` answers every step with what the data supports. The engine is pure.

Hidden by growth playbook (`src/playbooks/hiddenByGrowth.ts`, registry in `index.ts`: `playbookFor(type)` gives it for Hidden by growth and `null` for every other type): version 1.1, "Early hold release", six steps — Sales jump, Hidden rate, Card brands, Refunds, Growth explained?, Money. Hidden rate and Growth explained? are skipped when the jump answer is No. Each step's expected answer mirrors the matching flag check's thresholds (brands needs 30 judged payments per brand, else "Not enough data"; Growth explained? reads the seller's ad-receipts document). Its ending reads only confirmed flags: none critical or high → release all; risk and a hold above the worst case → partial release keeping the hold to keep; risk and a hold at or under the worst case → deny, keep all.

## 6. UI
Console view for working a case, a report view, and the Forge modal. Severity is always icon, word and color. Keyboard shortcuts drive flag review.

Console shell: `AppProvider` (`src/app/AppContext.tsx`) holds the reducer state, loads the saved cases and the analyst name when the app opens, and writes each changed case back 300 ms after the last change; if storage is blocked, or the first read or write fails, the app falls back to an in-memory store with the starters seeded there and a top-bar notice says the session won't be saved. Pending edits are also written when the page is hidden or closed (`pagehide`, `visibilitychange` to hidden) and on unmount. A stored code that no longer forges (for example one made by a newer generator) is listed as "Unreadable case" with its code and no severity chip, and opening it shows a short message in place of the console. The page follows the URL hash (`src/app/routes.ts`): `#/`, `#/case/<code>`, `#/case/<code>/report`, `#/forge`; a code that matches no saved case is ignored. The top bar has the brand, a case search (display id, seller name or code), a read-only Practice mode status that shows the open case's flag, and the analyst's initials. The case header shows the seller, the held amount, account age, last-30-day sales, growth against the prior month and a status (New, In progress, Decided). The left rail lists cases newest first with Open, Decided and All filters, each row showing display id, severity chip, title and held amount, then the case's data tabs (Disputes only for now, with its dispute count and its unresolved flag count). Icons come from one SVG sprite; severity chips pair icon, word and color.

Disputes tab (`src/ui/console/DisputesTab.tsx`, mounted in the centre column; the count mode, "By month of purchase" or "Last 30 days", lives in `App`): a plain-words sentence with live numbers, four KPI cards (last-30-day rate, struck through and marked MISLEADING while the hidden-by-growth flag is not dismissed; rate on judged sales with a bullet bar marked at Visa's 1.5% and Mastercard's 3.0%; Mastercard judged; refund rate), then an evidence grid that is 2-up from 1800px with equal-height cards: the month chart (judged bars solid, red at or above Visa's line, orange at 1%, grey below; too-new months hatched; filed-rate bars in Last 30 days mode), the brand table with the "If nothing changes" Mastercard fine ladder, why buyers disputed with outcomes and a tip, and every dispute newest first in a scrolling table. The selected flag's evidence ids (`month:YYYY-MM`, `brand:visa` / `brand:mastercard`, `kpi:refund-rate`, `reasons`) mark matching elements with `is-evidence`. Empty rates show an em dash. The `kpi:account-age` id marks the case header's Account age and Growth stats.

Flags and decision (right column; `FlagsPanel`, `ActivityLog`, `DecisionPanel`, shortcuts in `src/ui/useShortcuts.ts`): the header counts raised, confirmed, dismissed and to-review flags with a progress bar. The selected flag's card shows why it matters, Confirm, Dismiss and Evidence buttons and a note; confirmed or dismissed cards show their state with Undo. Evidence selects the flag, switches to its tab and scrolls the first matching evidence element into view. "Add my own flag" opens an inline form (title, severity, note); the new flag carries the selected flag's evidence, or none. The activity log lists the newest first with relative times (now, 5m, 2h, 3d). The decision panel is pinned at the bottom: the suggestion with its basis, a Hold in dollars (editable for partial release and approve-smaller-hold, applied on blur or Enter; `$`, commas and spaces are ignored; a value outside $0 to the held amount is clamped with "Kept within $0–<held>" under the field, and unreadable text keeps the old value with "Enter an amount in dollars"; the note clears on the next valid entry; shown as plain text for approve and the held-in-full actions: deny, hold payouts, escalate, block, refund buyers), the re-review date, an action select ("Use the suggestion" and every action), a Message preview of the seller letter and Build report. Once a case is decided the flag buttons, notes, Add flag, the decision select, hold and reason are disabled, the C and D shortcuts do nothing and no reason is asked for. Choosing a different action or hold than the suggestion requires a reason; Build report stays disabled with "Add a reason for changing the suggestion" until one is entered. In practice mode, until the case is decided, the suggestion is replaced by "Hidden in practice mode — make your own call", the select starts at "Choose a decision" and Build report waits for a decision. Shortcuts: J/K move, C confirm, D dismiss, E evidence, R open the report, Ctrl+K focus search; none but Ctrl+K fire while typing in an input, textarea, select or editable element. Flag titles are buttons, so cards select from the keyboard. A decision is logged only when its action or kept amount changes, and the line mentions a reason only when one was entered. In practice mode a partial release starts from half the held amount (to the nearest $1,000) or the analyst's own hold, never from the suggestion.

Report page (`src/ui/report/`, route `#/case/<code>/report`; it replaces the console's three columns and the top bar shows the breadcrumbs "Cases / <id> / Report" and Back to case): a left rail with a contents list (buttons that scroll to each section), case at a glance, every flag with a shared badge column, the attached evidence and a "Same facts, same answer" panel (earlier decisions as kept-percent bars and an in-line verdict; in practice mode it reads "Revealed after you decide" until the case is decided); severity chips in the flags list carry the severity icon; a white paper with the header, the Recommendation (release, keep, review date, and an override box naming the rules' suggestion and the analyst's reason when they differ), 1 What we found (confirmed findings and the dismissed row in one badge column), 2 Money at risk (table, coverage bar, how it was estimated), 3 the release conditions and fallback, 4 the seller message, 5 Fix for everyone, 6 Audit trail (the saved activity, with one "Rules v<version> suggested" row after "Checks ran" unless the suggestion is hidden) and a footer; and a right rail with sign-off, the second reviewer, the editable seller message with a word count, the Before you send checks, Export (PDF and Print both use the browser's print dialog, JSON downloads a local file) and Mark case decided. The print stylesheet hides the top bar and both rails. Second review: from the held amount in `RULES_V1.secondReviewAt` a reviewer name is required; below it "Ask anyway" reveals an optional field. Mark case decided is enabled only while the case is not yet decided and all four checks pass (every flag reviewed, a reason when the decision differs from the suggestion, the kept amount covers the worst case for a partial release, seller message ticked as read) and any required reviewer name is filled. The JSON export is `{ code, displayId, rulesVersion, decision, flags, report }`. In practice mode a case with no decision yet shows only "Choose a decision in the console first" and Back to case; until Mark case decided the page omits the override box and every mention of the rules' suggestion, and Mark case decided saves the decision shown (when none was saved), makes the case read-only (the reviewer name, seller message, read tick and every console edit are disabled and ignored by the reducer), scores the case and swaps Before you send for the Practice result: the total out of 100, Flags 40, Dismissals 15, Decision 30 and Amounts 15 as bars, what was missed (or "Nothing — clean run.") and "The rules would have suggested: <summary>". A Surprise case keeps its type hidden until it is decided: the list and case header read "Unknown case", the report title reads "Unknown case — <seller>", and the rail's Same facts, same answer panel shows "Revealed after you decide"; once decided everything shows normally.

Forge modal (`src/ui/forge/`, opened by the rail's New case button or the `#/forge` route; Cancel, ×, Escape or a click on the dimmed backdrop closes it and returns `#/forge` to the open case): a 3×4 grid of the 12 case types (`caseTypeCards.ts`) with tag filter chips (All 12, Statements, Transactions, Disputes, Owners, Payouts). Only Hidden by growth and Surprise me can be picked; the rest are dimmed, marked Coming soon and ignore clicks. On the right, Difficulty and Seller size selectors, the case code (typing a code updates the type, difficulty and size; a mistyped code or a coming-soon type shows its message under the field and disables Forge case; the dice, and picking Surprise me, make a new seed and Copy puts the code on the clipboard) and a Practice mode switch (on by default). "What you'll get" is computed by forging the current code: payments, disputes, hold under review, hidden warning signs, innocent look-alikes, time to solve (about 6 / 10 / 15 minutes for easy / normal / hard) and the story; for Surprise me every row reads "Hidden until you investigate". Forge case (or Enter) opens the case if its code is already in the list, otherwise adds it with the two opening activity lines (`src/app/opening.ts`: opened by the Forge, and how many flags the checks raised), goes to `#/case/<code>` and closes. While the modal is open its keystrokes don't reach the console shortcuts.

## 7. Persistence
Cases and decisions are stored locally in IndexedDB; nothing leaves the browser.

`src/persist/db.ts` opens the `risk-forge` database (version 1) through `idb`: a `cases` store keyed by `code` and a `settings` store (one record, default analyst name "Analyst"). A `CaseState` holds only what the analyst did (flag states and notes, analyst-added flags, decision and override reason, seller message, second reviewer, activity log, decided time, score); the case itself is re-forged from its code. If IndexedDB cannot be opened, `openStore` returns an in-memory store with `persistent: false` so the app keeps working for the session.

First run: when the store holds no cases at all, the app creates the three starter cases (`STARTER_CODES`: Easy, Normal, Hard; practice off, each with the two opening activity lines, opened 1 ms apart so Hard is newest), saves them and opens the Hard one unless the URL already names a saved case. It never seeds again once any case exists, even if all are removed later; an empty store is the only trigger.

App state lives in `src/app/state.ts`, a pure reducer over the case list, the active case, view, tab, selected flag, Forge modal, search and analyst name. Case-scoped actions only touch the active case. Activity lines are logged for flag state changes (confirmed, dismissed, reopened), analyst-added flags, decision changes when the action or the kept amount changes, and sign-off (signed the report, marked decided). Notes, the seller message, the reviewer name, the read tick and reason-only edits log nothing. A decision's `keep` is clamped to 0..held and rounded to whole cents, and `release` is held minus keep. Once `decidedAt` is set, every edit action is a no-op for that case. Times in the audit trail and the sign-off are labelled UTC, as is the Opened date in the case header.

## 8. Determinism & case codes
Generators use only a seeded RNG, so a case code reproduces the same case. Generator v1 uses asOf 2026-11-08.

The RNG is mulberry32 (`src/lib/rng.ts`); its sequence is frozen for generator v1. Case codes use Crockford base32 (`src/lib/base32.ts`) with a weighted mod-31 check character; entry is normalized (uppercase, whitespace, hyphens, dashes, `_` and `.` stripped, I/L read as 1, O as 0). A case code (`src/domain/caseCode.ts`) is 8 characters shown as `XX-XXXX-XX`: a 35-bit value (11-bit header of version, case type, difficulty and size, XOR-masked by an RNG draw seeded from the case seed, followed by the 24-bit seed) in 7 base32 characters plus the check character. Decoding rejects wrong length, characters outside the alphabet, check-character mismatches, and codes from a newer generator version. The display id is `RF-` plus the last four digits of the seed. Money is integer cents and dates are UTC ISO strings (`src/lib/money.ts`, `src/lib/dates.ts`).

## 9. Testing & QA
`npm test` runs the full suite, `npm run typecheck` checks types, and `npm run build` produces the static bundle. `npm run check:offline` builds and fails if anything in `dist` points at a remote address (a web URL other than the allow-listed `www.w3.org`, `react.dev/errors` and `reactjs.org` string constants, a `<link>` with a remote href, or `@import url(http`). `npm run qa:shots` builds, serves the bundle with `vite preview` on port 4317, and saves Chrome headless screenshots of the console, report and Forge screens at 2512×1398 and 1440×960 into `qa-shots/` (each with a fresh browser profile, so the first-run starters appear; `CHROME_PATH` overrides the Chrome location); the preview server is always stopped afterwards.
